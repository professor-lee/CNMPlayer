## 10. Local data and privacy

### 10.1 What can remain on the device

Depending on configuration and usage, local data includes:

- `auth/session.toml` in the asset root: the persisted login cookie and its update time. The cookie is
  serialized as readable TOML, **not encrypted at rest by this file format**. File permissions are not
  encryption and do not protect against every privileged process, backup system or compromised device.
- `playback/session.toml`: remembered queue and playback state when playback memory is enabled.
- `private_roam/session.toml`: private-roam tracks, position and related metadata.
- Configuration and themes, cached audio and artwork, and separate downloaded files with embedded tags.
- Local application logs, including `Player.log` and, on Unix, `Player.stderr.log`; inspect actual logs
  for sensitive identifiers, paths or request details before sharing them.

Asset, cache and download directories can be different and can be overridden. Use the
[README's storage description](README.md#first-run-and-asset-root) and your actual configuration to
locate them rather than assume that all data lives in one directory.

### 10.2 Local does not mean private or anonymous

Filenames, listening records, artwork, terminal output and screenshots can reveal interests or account
information. Operating-system media integration, such as MPRIS, can expose playback metadata to other
applications allowed to access that interface. Device administrators, other users with access, backups,
cloud-sync tools and third-party utilities may also read local information.

Protect the device and directories, avoid committing generated state to repositories, and redact
reports before publication. This notice is not a promise that all data is encrypted, automatically
redacted, anonymous, inaccessible to third parties or covered by a complete privacy policy for every
environment in which someone deploys the software.
